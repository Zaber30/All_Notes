. Web Exploitation (Website Hacking)

- **What it is:** Finding flaws in websites, web apps, and databases.
- **The Goal:** Break past login screens, steal user data, or deface the website.
- **💡 Simple Analogy:** The front door of a bank is locked, but you find a window in the back that someone forgot to latch.
- **Key Concept:** You are manipulation how data moves between a web browser and a server.

2. Reverse Engineering (Software Decoding)

- **What it is:** Taking a finished program or app and breaking it down backward to see how it was built.
- **The Goal:** Find hidden passwords inside the code, bypass activation keys, or figure out how malware works.
- **💡 Simple Analogy:** Buying a secret-recipe cake from a bakery, taking it home to a lab, and figuring out every exact ingredient used to bake it.
- **Key Concept:** Reading compiled code (like Assembly) when you don't have the original source code.

3. Binary Exploitation / Pwn (System Hacking)

- **What it is:** Crashing or tricking a computer program's memory to make it do something it wasn't supposed to do.
- **The Goal:** Force a server or software to give you full administrative access (called "getting root").
- **💡 Simple Analogy:** Overfilling a vending machine coin slot so heavily that the internal computer glitched and drops all the snacks for free.
- **Key Concept:** Exploiting memory mistakes (like Buffer Overflows) in programs written in C or C++.

4. Network Hacking (Infrastructure Hacking)

- **What it is:** Attacking the "highways" and routers that connect computers together.
- **The Goal:** Intercept private data flying through the air (Wi-Fi) or trick devices into sending data to you instead of the real router.
- **💡 Simple Analogy:** Becoming a fake mailman, intercepting letters before they reach the real post office, reading them, and taping them back up.
- **Key Concept:** Listening to network traffic and manipulating internet protocols.

5. Cryptography (Code Breaking)

- **What it is:** Attacking the math used to encrypt and lock secret data.
- **The Goal:** Decrypt secret messages without being given the actual password or key.
- **💡 Simple Analogy:** Finding a secret diary that is written in a scrambled secret code, and studying the patterns until you figure out what alphabet was used.
- **Key Concept:** Finding mathematical mistakes made by developers when they tried to lock up data.

6. Cloud Hacking (Modern Infrastructure)

- **What it is:** Attacking servers hosted on virtual platforms like Amazon AWS or Google Cloud.
- **The Goal:** Find digital storage folders that companies accidentally left public to steal massive company databases.
- **💡 Simple Analogy:** A business rents a storage locker in a massive public facility, but forgets to put a padlock on their specific locker door.
- **Key Concept:** Targeting cloud permissions and misconfigurations rather than coding bugs.

7. Hardware & IoT Hacking (Device Hacking)

- **What it is:** Hacking physical gadgets like smart TVs, smart fridges, routers, or electronic door locks.
- **The Goal:** Extract the internal code directly from the physical chips inside the device to bypass security.
- **💡 Simple Analogy:** Unscrewing the back of an electronic safe and manually hotwiring the circuit board to make the lock pop open.
- **Key Concept:** Dealing with physical electronics and embedded firmware.

8. Social Engineering (Human Hacking)

- **What it is:** Hacking the _people_ operating the computers instead of hacking the computers themselves.
- **The Goal:** Trick an employee into typing their password into a fake login screen or giving secrets away over the phone.
- **💡 Simple Analogy:** Putting on a high-visibility vest and a fake ID badge, walking past security by pretending to be the "AC repair guy," and walking straight into the server room.
- **Key Concept:** Exploiting human trust, fear, or helpfulness (Phishing).
